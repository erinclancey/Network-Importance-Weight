# Network Importance on Disease Transmission

Contact networks are widely recognized as critical to understanding and controlling the spread of infectious diseases in human and animal populations. Despite the recognized importance of heterogeneous contact patterns, random-mixing models remain the predominant framework in epidemiological and public health modeling due to the scarcity of paired network and disease surveillance data and the mathematical and computational complexity of network-based approaches. Consequently, it is often unclear whether new or ongoing collection of network data is warranted for improving scientific understanding or informing public health decisions. To address this challenge, we develop a robust statistical framework to assess network importance on disease transmission. First, we outline mathematical and statistical models to incorporate a network-weight parameter into a contact matrix that can be estimated from epidemiological data. Using synthetic case-reporting data, we then evaluate the statistical performance of the proposed methodology. Finally, we develop a Bayesian framework for quantifying network importance by comparing epidemic outcomes under the inferred network structure and a random-mixing counterfactual. Overall, our method is straightforward to implement and provides a practical tool for guiding disease surveillance and assessing the extent to which network data improve the prediction and control of infectious disease spread.
